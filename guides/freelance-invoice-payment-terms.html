Late payments are the most common cash problem freelancers have. Most of it comes down to three things: vague terms, invoices sent late, and no follow-up routine. All three are fixable.

Pick short, specific terms

  • Net 14 is a good default for small clients. Net 30 is normal for larger companies, which often can't go faster.
  • Write the actual due date on the invoice ("Due 14 November"), not just "Net 14". People act on dates.
  • For new clients or big projects, ask for 25% to 50% upfront.

Put these on every invoice

  • A unique invoice number, so you can both refer to it
  • The client's PO number, if they use them, since many finance teams can't pay without one
  • Exactly how to pay you, including bank details or a payment link
  • Your late-fee policy, if you have one and it's in your contract

Invoice the same day

The payment clock only starts when the invoice arrives. Invoice the day you deliver, or on a fixed date each month for retainers.

A follow-up routine that isn't awkward

  1. 3 days before due: a friendly reminder with the invoice attached.
  2. 1 day after due: "Just checking this reached the right person."
  3. 7 days overdue: ask directly when payment will be made, and copy their accounts team.
  4. 14+ days overdue: pause work and apply late fees as your contract allows.

The routine only works if you know what's due and what's late at a glance. Keep every invoice in one tracker with issue date, due date and amount paid, and check it weekly.
